# PHIL 201 — September 8, 2026

## Summary

This course focuses on ancient Greek philosophy, emphasizing critical thinking, logical analysis, and practical application through a trifold model (summary, analysis, synthesis). Philosophy is defined as 'love of wisdom' requiring justification and systematic reasoning, distinct from mere theory or quotes. The course uses the textbook 'Ancient Philosophy' by Julia Annis and integrates real-world applications like ethical decision-making and dialogue practices.

## Key Points

- Concept — Philosophy: 'Love of wisdom' (philos + sophia), requiring critical engagement, systematic reasoning, and justification of claims, not just quotes or theories.
- Concept — Trifold Model: Assignments structured as summary (brief overview), analysis (systematic reasoning), and synthesis (real-world application, e.g., medical ethics).
- Concept — Capital D Dialogue: Deep, meaningful exchange beyond ordinary conversation, requiring humility, active listening, and intellectual courage to 'look stupid' for growth.
- Concept — Metaphysics: Study of what exists, including pre-Socratic theories (e.g., Parmenides, four classical elements), essential nature (chicken nugget example), and being vs. potential.
- Concept — Philosophy's Scope: Ancient Greek traditions (not Eastern), with focus on logical forms (syllogisms), ethical applications, and practical tools like 'How to Focus' and 'How to Win an Argument'.
- Concept — Synthesis: Applying concepts to real-world scenarios (e.g., 'what would I do as a physician?'), avoiding overcomplication, and using thought experiments.
- Concept — Epistemology: Next week's focus on how knowledge is acquired, via Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy or Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy.
- Concept — Hard vs. Soft Determinism: Hard determinism (external agents determine outcomes) vs. soft determinism (future states depend solely on prior states).
- Concept — Aristotelian Logic: Deductive reasoning, syllogisms, and emphasis on avoiding excessive examples in analysis.
- Concept — Pneuma: Greek term for soul, linking to breath and life (e.g., Adam receiving life through breath in biblical contexts).
- Concept — Capital D Dialogue Rules: Brevity, seeking the good, active listening, and minimal distractions to foster mutual understanding.
- Concept — Pre-Socratic Philosophy: Early theories on existence (e.g., water, air as fundamental elements), distinct from Socrates' ethical focus.
- Big takeaway — This course focuses on ancient Greek philosophy, emphasizing critical thinking, logical analysis, and practical application through a trifold model (summary, analysis, synthesis).
