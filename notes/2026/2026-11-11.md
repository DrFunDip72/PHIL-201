## Summary

A student receives a Hartwell Foundation letter with a $1,000 check and a sealed envelope predicting their action: if they return the check, the envelope contains a $200,000 scholarship; if they keep both, the envelope is empty. The model has 99% accuracy over 5,000 students, but the student must choose between keeping the envelope alone ($200,000) or both items ($1,000 + empty envelope). The lecture explores the decision's implications, emphasizing the model's prediction is fixed and cannot be altered by current actions.

## Key Points

- Hartwell Foundation sends $1,000 check and sealed envelope to students who haven't paid enrollment deposit
- Sealed envelope contains $200,000 if student returns check (keeps envelope), otherwise empty
- Model predicts student actions with 99% accuracy (4,950 correct out of 5,000 students)
- Model uses transcripts, essays, writing samples, and form choices to predict decisions
- Student must choose: keep envelope alone ($200,000) or both items ($1,000 + empty envelope) by deadline
- Envelope sealed three weeks ago; prediction is fixed and cannot be changed by student decisions
- Decision has no causal impact on envelope contents (sealed prior to decision)
- Lecture uses classroom experiment showing 50-50 split in choices with 99% model accuracy
- Scholarship value requires student to have paid $1,000 enrollment deposit by Friday
- Hartwell Foundation is independent of student's school and provides scholarship separately
- Big takeaway — A student receives a Hartwell Foundation letter with a $1,000 check and a sealed envelope predicting their action: if they return the check, the envelope contains a $200,000 scholarship; if they keep both, the envelope is empty.
