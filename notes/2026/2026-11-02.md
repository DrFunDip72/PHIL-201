The lecture also discusses infinite regress, the ontological argument, and Aquinas' responses to objections such as the problem of evil and entropy, while clarifying Aquinas' concept of infinity distinct from modern mathematics.

## Key Points

- Aquinas' Summa Theologica, Part I, Question 2, Article 3 focuses on proofs for God's existence
- Catholic doctrine is defined as what is revealed in Jesus Christ, including scripture and tradition
- Aquinas presents five distinct ways to prove God's existence, with Article 3 addressing whether God exists
- God is defined as simple (no parts), impassable (not affected by emotions), self-existent (independent of external factors), and necessary (not contingent on external causes)
- The first of Aquinas' five proofs is the argument for motion, identifying God as the unmoved mover that causes all motion
- The argument from gradation shows imperfection in the world implies a perfect standard (God) as the ultimate source of perfection
- Aquinas' concept of God as love involves willing the good of the object of love, which can cause pain without emotional affect
- Infinite regress is rejected by Aquinas as circular explanations, contrasting with the turtle analogy where each turtle stands on another
- Anselm's ontological argument defines God as 'that of which no greater can be conceived,' asserting existence as a necessary perfection
- Aquinas addresses the problem of evil by distinguishing between types of infinity, showing evil does not limit God's infiniteness
- Aquinas rejects infinite time because time is traversed finitely, as a finite entity cannot traverse an infinite distance
- Counting numbers are considered a potential actual infinity by Aquinas, as they increase through a relationship to themselves
